## 1. The model actually does the task
A circuit claim is meaningless if the behaviour is not there to begin with.

| scheme | clean logit diff | corrupted logit diff | IO beats S (clean) | IO is top-1 (clean) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `s2_swap` | +3.697 | -3.599 | 100.0% | 93.0% |
| `abc` | +3.697 | -0.188 | 100.0% | 93.0% |

Logit difference is `logit(IO) - logit(S)` at the final token, with IO and S fixed to the *clean* prompt's names in all runs so the three numbers are comparable. All patching results are normalized onto that span: **0.0 = corrupted behaviour, 1.0 = clean behaviour restored**.

Values above 1.0 appear below and are not errors. Splicing a name mover's clean output into the corrupted run can make the model prefer the indirect object *more* strongly than it does on the clean prompt itself, because the patched run keeps the corrupted context while gaining the clean answer signal. This is why the narrowing in section 6 targets closeness to 1.0 rather than maximum recovery.

## 2. Corruption schemes, and what each one can see
Two counterfactuals were run, because each is blind to something the other sees.

- **`s2_swap`** — the repeated subject name is swapped for the indirect object, flipping the correct answer. Exactly one token differs, which makes the metric cleanly symmetric. The cost: clean and corrupted activations are *identical* at every position before S2, so patching there is a mathematical no-op and no component acting earlier than S2 can be detected at all.
- **`abc`** — all three name slots are replaced with fresh names, so no name repeats. Activations differ from the first name onward, which makes early positions visible, at the cost of a corrupted run whose logit difference sits near zero rather than symmetrically negative.

The blindness of `s2_swap` is measured, not assumed. Across the full head sweep, the number of (head, position) cells whose effect is *exactly* zero:

| position | `s2_swap` exact zeros | `abc` exact zeros | precedes S2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| IO | 144/144 | 12/144 | yes |
| IO+1 | 144/144 | 12/144 | yes |
| S1 | 144/144 | 12/144 | yes |
| S1+1 | 144/144 | 12/144 | yes |
| S2 | 12/144 | 12/144 | no |
| S2+1 | 12/144 | 12/144 | no |
| END | 0/144 | 0/144 | no |

Every head is an exact zero at every position before S2 under `s2_swap` — a floating-point exact zero, because the two runs are computing on identical inputs there. Any published head whose role is at those positions cannot be found by this scheme even in principle.

The residual 12 zeros at each non-END position, present under both schemes, are a second and unrelated structural zero: they are the final layer's heads, whose output nothing reads except the logits at END, so patching them anywhere else cannot change the prediction. Both patterns falling exactly where the architecture says they must is a check that the hooks write where they claim to.

## 6. Iterative narrowing to a joint circuit
A ranking of marginal effects is not a circuit: heads can be redundant, so a set chosen by individual scores can overshoot or undershoot. Each step below adds the single node that brings recovery *closest to 1.0* given everything already patched, stopping when the best remaining addition closes less than 0.5% of the gap.

## 7. What this validated, and what it did not
Taking the union of both corruption schemes at the headline cutoff, 20/26 published heads were recovered (8 heads outside the published circuit also cleared it).

**Validated.** The behaviour is real and localized: patching single attention heads moves the logit difference by a large fraction of its full span, and the heads that do so are concentrated in the layers and positions the paper predicts.

**Not validated, by construction.** Three limits are structural, not tuning problems:

1. **Activation patching is not path patching.** The published circuit was derived with *path* patching, which measures a component's effect along a specific downstream route. Plain activation patching measures a node's total effect on the output. A head whose contribution is routed entirely through another head can therefore be invisible here even though it is genuinely in the circuit. Classes defined by their *indirect* role — previous token, duplicate token, induction — are the ones this hits hardest.

2. **`s2_swap` cannot see anything before S2.** With one differing token, clean and corrupted activations coincide at every earlier position, so previous-token heads acting at S1+1 have an effect of *exactly* zero — not a weak signal, a structurally undefined one. `abc` was added specifically to cover this, and is the only scheme that can speak to those heads.

3. **Marginal effects understate redundant components.** The paper's backup name movers exist precisely because they activate when the primary name movers are removed. Patching one head at a time, with everything else intact, is the condition under which redundant components look least important.

None of these are reasons to discount the misses; they are the reasons the misses are where they are, and they are the concrete argument for implementing path patching before this method is pointed at a circuit nobody has published.
